<Info>
  **Padel Fan Index** measures the real popularity of professional padel players among active padel fans. It is available through the API with `GET /api/players/{id}/audience`, and as custom reports for brands, sponsors and players.
</Info>

## Why It's Different

Traditional sponsorship metrics rely on passive impressions: social media followers, TV viewership estimates, or event attendance. Padel Fan Index measures something far more valuable: **active fan decisions**.

Every data point represents a deliberate action by a fan: choosing a player for their team before a tournament, over every other player available. These are signals of genuine preference that passive metrics cannot capture.

---

## Audience Endpoint

`GET /api/players/{id}/audience` returns the popularity of any player at any time: how many fans choose them, how they compare with the rest of the circuit and with their own ranking, and how that popularity evolves.

<Note>
  This endpoint [requires a **Business** subscription](https://padelapi.org/billing).
</Note>

Every value is relative: shares, indexes and positions against the rest of the circuit. The endpoint never returns absolute fan counts, so values compare fairly across players and over time.

The endpoint measures a window: the last 12 months by default, ending today. Set `after_date` and `before_date` (`YYYY-MM-DD`) to measure any other window. Every value in the response is computed over that same window.

```bash
curl 'https://padelapi.org/api/players/65/audience' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer YOUR_API_TOKEN'
```

### Popularity

`fan_share`, `popularity_index` and `popularity_rank` describe the player at the latest tournament of the window, so the most recent entry of `by_period` and the top level tell the same story. `fan_reach` and `fan_retention` cover the whole window.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `from`, `to` | The window actually measured, so you know what the defaults resolved to. |
| `fan_share` | Percentage of active fans who chose the player at the latest tournament of the window. |
| `fan_reach` | Percentage of the fans active during the window who chose the player at least once. Where `fan_share` is a snapshot of one tournament, `fan_reach` is the breadth of the audience over the whole window: a player many fans try for a tournament or two reaches far beyond their share. |
| `popularity_index` | Player's fans divided by the average of every player chosen at that tournament. `2.0` means twice as popular as the average player. |
| `popularity_rank` | Position of the player by number of fans at that tournament. |
| `popularity_gap` | Official ranking minus `popularity_rank`. Positive means the player attracts more fans than their competitive level would predict. |
| `fan_retention` | Percentage of fans who kept choosing the player from one tournament to the next across the window. |

`fan_share`, `fan_reach`, `popularity_index`, `popularity_rank` and `fan_retention` are `null` when no fan chose the player within the window. `popularity_gap` is also `null` when the player has no official ranking.

### Evolution

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `by_period` | One entry per calendar month within the window, oldest first, each with its `from` and `to` dates. Months where no fan chose the player are left out. |
| `by_period[].fan_share` | Average share of fans who chose the player across the tournaments of that month. |
| `by_period[].new_fan_rate` | Percentage of that month's fans who chose the player for the first time ever. High values signal a player who is reaching new audiences. `null` in the month the player first appears, where every fan is new by definition. |
| `by_period[].fan_retention` | Percentage of fans who kept the player from one tournament to the next within that month. |
| `trend` | Direction of `fan_share` across `by_period`: `rising`, `stable` or `declining`. It is relative to the player's own level, so a move from 2% to 3% counts as much as one from 20% to 30%. `null` when fewer than three periods are available. |

Retention is measured between consecutive tournaments of the same season. The first tournament of a season has no previous one to compare with, because fans build their teams again when a new season starts.

## Data Sources

Padel Fan Index combines two proprietary data sources:

  <Card title="Fantasy Padel Tour" icon="gamepad" href="https://fantasypadeltour.com">
